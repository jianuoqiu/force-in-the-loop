1In-domain closed-loop evaluation: a four-rung ladder

1 · Expert commands, exact replay. Recorded motor targets, no force law. Infrastructure check.

2 · Perfect forecast. No network: the recording's own future measured states, forces and contacts as the chunk. Ceiling of this reference type.

3 · Policy on recorded observations. Real checkpoint, inputs from the recording, forecasts executed live. Teacher forcing.

4 · Policy live. The real closed loop, same start.

Settings
  • Episodes: 5 training episodes per task (seeds 1000–1079). 4 tasks × 5 episodes × 7 conditions = 140 rollouts; every reset bit-identical to the recording.
  • Policy: Week 3 checkpoints unchanged; point cloud in the videos, RGB in the tables. Rungs 1 and 2 use no network.
  • Loop: forecast every 2 states, linear interpolation at 120 Hz, velocity feedforward, task-space force law (kp 0.3, ki 10, clip 0.12 rad). Rung 1: recorded PD targets, hold interpolation, null law.
  • Rungs 2 and 3: forecasts end with the recording; the last reference is held 2 s for a verdict.
  • End: success = all sub-goals + retract; otherwise 10 s no-progress timeout.

2Why the live rollout drifts

Rungs 3 and 4: same checkpoint, same start, only the source of the policy's inputs differs. Traced state by state on beam step 1, seed 1000 (no contact, zero force throughout).

Key points

  • State 0: identical observation → identical forecast (2×10−7 rad) → identical simulator state through state 2. Policy deterministic; deploy-side inputs equal the training-side computation.
  • But the first move is not the expert's: at reset every non-visual input is the same in every episode of a task, and the network ignores the point cloud. State-0 arm forecast identical across 12 training episodes (spread 0.000 rad) while the true first moves spread 0.065 rad. It outputs the dataset-average first move; cosine −0.34 with the expert here.
  • State 2, the only difference: rung 3 is shown the recording's state, rung 4 its own simulator's state.
  • The velocity input amplifies it: swap test at state 4, cloud and joint positions fixed. Recorded velocity → arm forecast cosine +0.84 with the expert; live velocity → −0.08. Swapping the joint positions instead changes it by 0.03.
  • Net: rung 3 looks normal only because it is told every 2 frames that it is where the expert was. Rung 4 follows its own velocity instead of the scene.

3Actuation channel: position offset vs feedforward torque

Force so far = command the joint past the contact, so the servo's tracking error becomes the squeeze (dq = −K−1JTf). Objection: position control is the wrong channel for a closed force loop. Test: the same commanded force delivered as joint torque (τ = −JTf).

Settings
  • Episodes: 12 perfect expert episodes, 3 per task; 2 conditions = 24 rollouts, one cold-start process each.
  • Reference: oracle chunk policy re-planned every 12 frames from the current state, so both arms share it only until their control diverges. Measured joint states, so the law must supply the preload (rung 2 of 1).
  • Position arm: task-space law, kp 0.3, ki 10, offset clip 0.12 rad.
  • Torque arm: same commanded force as joint torque, clipped at the servo limits (0.4 to 3.3 N·m); position target keeps the reference + contact-point channel only.

Same actuator, different limit

  • Implicit PD torque = K(q* − q) + τff. Position adds K·dq = −JTf, torque adds −JTf: identical commanded torque.
  • Only the saturation differs: the 0.12 rad offset clip is a per-joint torque cap of 0.11 N·m on the distal joints, vs a servo limit up to 3.3 N·m.
  • Lifting the position clip delivers +8.50 N at a 20 N request vs +8.77 N for torque: the gap is the clip, not the channel.
  • Both channels deliver only 0.4 to 0.5 N per newton asked, even unsaturated.

  • The channel is not the bottleneck: torque loses the three beam-step-1 episodes position wins, and that is the whole gap. Torque has the smallest force bias of any setting so far: best force tracking, worst task.
  • As the mechanism predicts: a wider clip only buys more squeeze, and wider clips were already measured harmful in Weeks 1–2 (clip 0.20 rad: 0 of 12).
  • One lead: screwing seed 0, torque 6 of 10 sub-goals with the part held; position 0 and dropped. Single episode.
  • Off-simulator the objection stands: stiff hardware servos, bandwidth, no torque interface. These simulated servos already act as a compliant force source.
  • The real limit is unchanged: a per-fingertip force law cannot reconstruct a preload the reference never contained (1, rung 2).

5Per-task action policies: the expert's RL actions, no force controller

Same model, inputs and training starts as the point-cloud policies of 6, but the target is the expert's next 16 RL actions (the commands, not measured states). They are executed exactly as the expert's were: through play2perfect's own action pipeline, with no middle layer and no force law.

Settings
  • Checkpoints: 4 per-task EgoVerse HPT flow-matching policies, point cloud + the usual state inputs, trained 21.2k steps on that task's 90 bc_dataset_small training episodes. Output: a[s…s+15], 29-dim in [−1, 1] (7 arm + 22 hand).
  • Execution: forecast every 2 states; each frame the chunk's action for that state goes through the env's pipeline: arm target += 1.5 rad/s × dt × a, then EMA 0.1; hand = EMA 0.1 of the action mapped onto the joint range. Both physics substeps hold that target. Mean of 128 samples × 5 Euler steps per forecast.

  • Rung 1 here: the recorded actions through the same path reproduce every recording exactly (0.000 rad, 0.0 mm): 20/20.
  • Episodes: the same 20 training starts as 6 (seeds 5000–5117), so the columns compare directly with the state-forecast policy.

The policy under test

A feed-forward behaviour-cloning network, one model for all four tasks. From one camera observation, the measured robot state and a task ID it predicts a chunk of 16 future measured states: joint positions, fingertip force vectors and contacts. It outputs references for the controller, not motor commands; it was never trained on expert actions.

Given a recorded observation, the policy forecasts the next 267 ms: where every joint will be, which fingertips will be in contact, where on the fingertip, and with what force vector. What the rollouts show. The part is never grasped. Within the first second the object height drops by 8 to 10 cm in every episode: the hand does not close fast enough, the part slips out, and the rest of the episode is spent near the table. Where the error is. The controller follows the policy's reference closely: 6 mrad mean hand error, under 1 mrad on the arm, and the force law tracks its targets to within 1 N. The reference itself is wrong. From rest the policy under-predicts how fast the hand should close, and once forecasts build on the policy's own states the arm reference drifts 0.18 rad from the recording within 0.65 s. This is closed-loop covariate shift of the BC policy. The offline under-prediction of firm grasps in Result 1 points the same way: the force targets the law is asked to track are themselves too small.

Checks that passed. Replaying the recorded commands through the same live pipeline completes the tasks, and the live policy inputs match the dataset files bit for bit, so the wiring is not the cause. The velocity feedforward halves the arm tracking error but does not change the outcome.
